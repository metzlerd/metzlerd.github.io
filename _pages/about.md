I'm a Senior Staff Research Scientist at [Google DeepMind](https://deepmind.google/),
where my current focus is pre-training data quality for Gemini.

My research is on large language models: how they are pretrained, how their outputs can
be detected and evaluated, and how they store and retrieve knowledge. Much of it builds
on two decades of earlier work on retrieval and ranking, which is where a good deal of
the machinery behind today's systems originated. I am a member of the
[SIGIR Academy](https://sigir.org/awards/sigir-academy/) and a Distinguished Member of
the ACM.

**35,000+ citations** · **h-index 61** · **Two books** · **14 patents** · **3 Test of Time awards**
{: .notice--info}

## Selected work

**Large language models.** Co-author of
[Emergent Abilities of Large Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.07682)
(7,300+ citations), the [UL2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.05131),
[ExT5](https://arxiv.org/abs/2111.10952), and [Charformer](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.12672)
pretraining models, the [Long Range Arena](https://arxiv.org/abs/2011.04006) benchmark,
and [Efficient Transformers: A Survey](https://arxiv.org/abs/2009.06732). UL2 was
released as open weights in 2022. I contributed to the
[Gemini 2.5](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.06261) technical report.

**Generative retrieval.** [Rethinking Search: Making Domain Experts out of Dilettantes](https://arxiv.org/abs/2105.02274)
(2021) made the case for replacing the classic index-and-rank pipeline with a single
model, and [Transformer Memory as a Differentiable Search Index](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.06991)
(NeurIPS 2022) showed it could actually work. Together they helped establish generative
retrieval as a research area; I went on to co-organize the first two Gen-IR workshops at
SIGIR 2023 and 2024.

**Personal search.** Research on learning to rank from the sparse, biased feedback that
personal search produces, recognized with the **SIGIR 2026 Test of Time award**. The work
shipped across Gmail and Drive through more than 100 quality launches.

**Evaluation and retrieval foundations.** Three separate threads have aged well:
[Expected Reciprocal Rank](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/1645953.1646033), developed at
Yahoo! Research and now a standard measure for graded relevance (**CIKM 2021 Test of
Time**); similarity measures for short segments of text, developed at Microsoft Research
(**ECIR 2017 Test of Time**); and the Markov Random Field model for term dependencies
(SIGIR 2005 Best Student Paper, SIGIR 2017 Test of Time honorable mention), alongside
work on the Indri search engine.

**Teaching the field.** Co-author of
[Search Engines: Information Retrieval in Practice](https://ciir.cs.umass.edu/irbook/),
a widely used undergraduate and graduate information retrieval textbook that is freely
available online, and author of
[A Feature-Centric View of Information Retrieval](https://link.springer.com/book/10.1007/978-3-642-22898-8)
(Springer, 2011).
